## 1. URINARY TRACT INFECTION


**Renal physiological changes in pregnancy**
- **Anatomical**
	The **anatomical changes in the urinary/renal system during pregnancy**  are:
1. **Dilatation of the calyces, renal pelvis and ureters** — producing hydroureter and hydronephrosis.
2. **Increase in kidney size** — the kidney increases by approximately **1 cm in length** during normal pregnancy.
3. **Upward and anterior displacement of the bladder** by the enlarging uterus.
4. **Hydroureter and hydronephrosis** — present in about **80% of women by the third trimester**.
5. **More pronounced dilatation of the right urinary tract** than the left.
6. **Increased urinary stasis** within the ureters.
7. **Increased vesicoureteric reflux (VUR)** during pregnancy.
### Why these changes occur
- **Mechanical compression by the enlarging uterus** is the principal cause of hydroureter and hydronephrosis.
- The **dextrorotation of the pregnant uterus** contributes to greater right-sided dilatation.
- Urinary stasis and increased vesicoureteric reflux increase the risk of **urinary tract infection and pyelonephritis**.
**Exam summary:**
> **Pregnancy → renal tract dilatation + kidney enlargement + bladder displacement + right-sided predominance + urinary stasis + increased VUR.**

### a. Asymptomatic Bacteriuria
Urine culture demonstrating the presence of bacteria in the urine in the absence of maternal symptoms of urinary tract infection

The **incidence** is the same in nonpregnant and pregnant females and averages **2-10%**;
#### Clinical Findings 
isolation of microorganisms with a colony count > 100, 000 organisms/ml of urine in a dean-catch specimen in a woman who is experiencing no symptoms of urinary tract infection. 

**Physiologic changes in pregnancy predispose a woman to bacteriuria, including:** 
- increased glucose concentration in the urine and 
- increased stasis due to the relaxant progesterone effect.

#### Risk factors 
- low socioeconomic status, 
- parity, 
- age, 
- sexual practice, and 
- medical conditions such as diabetes and sickle cell trait.

 **Common offending organism**
- Escherichia coli ( most common ~ 80% of cases ). 
- The Klebsiella-Enterobacter-Serratia group, 
- Staphylococcus aureus, 
- Enterococcus, 
- Group B Streptococcus,
- Proteus.

#### Complications 
- Pyelonephritis. 
- Preterm delivery, 
- Preeclampsia. 
- Fetal loss, and 
- Preterm birth and 
- Low birth-weight babies

#### Treatment
- Antibiotic x 5-14/7  ( initial empiric. E coli (MC), a sulfonamide, nitrofurantoin, cephalosporin is a reasonable choice)

### b. Acute Cystitis
Urine culture demonstrating the presence of bacteria in the urine in a patient symptomatic for urinary tract infection.

**incidence 1%**

#### Clinical Findings 
**Symptoms** 
- urinary frequency, 
- urgency, 
- dysuria, and 
- suprapubic discomfort. 

The urine often is 
- cloudy and 
- malodorous and 
- should be cultured to confirm the diagnosis and to identify antibiotic sensitivities. 

A colony count of 1000 confirms cystitis in a symptomatic patient

#### Treatment 
Treatment is the same as for asymptomatic bacteriuria. 
A urine culture should be repeated 1-2 weeks after therapy is started.

As with asymptomatic bacteriuria, suppressive therapy may be appropriate for women with UTI that persists after >= 2 courses of therapy. 
Nitrofurantoin (50-100 mg orally at bedtime) for the duration of the pregnancy is commonly used.
### c. Acute Pyelonephritis

**Incident of** 1-2% of all pregnant women

#### Clinical Findings 
- fever, shaking chills, 
- flank pain, 
- nausea and vomiting, 
- headache, 
- increased urinary frequency, and dysuria

- costovertebral angle tenderness, 

#### Investigation
- urinalysis (Pyuria)
- Urine mcs (Bacteruria, pyuria, wbc cast)
- urine bacteria count( 1-2/hpf  or >20/hpf in centrifuge urine )

#### Differential Diagnosis
-  appendicitis, 
- musculoskeletal pain, 
- costochondritis, 
- preterm labor, and 
- chorioamnionitis. 

#### Complications 
**Maternal** 
-  fever, 
- bacterial endotoxemia, 
- endotoxic shock, 
- renal insufficiency, 
- anemia, 
- leukocytosis, 
- thrombocytopenia, and 
- elevated fibrin split product levels. 

**Risk factors for recurrent or severe disease** 
-  history of pyelonephritis, 
- urinary tract malformation, or
- urinary calculi. 
 
The maternal anemia may be due to 
- marrow suppression, 
- increased erythrocyte destruction, or 
- diminished red cell production. 

- Pulmonary dysfunction (is association with acute pyelonephritis). 
	- Symptoms and signs may range from minimal 
		- mild cough and
		- slight pulmonary infiltrate) to 
		- severe (adult respiratory distress syndrome ). 
- Obstetrical complications include 
	- preterm labor and 
	- prematurity, 
	- fetal death, and 
	- intrauterine growth restriction.

#### Treatment
- Antibiotics(Cefazolin, ceftriazone) add gentamycin after 2-3days without improvement( cause by Urolithiasis, urinary tract abnormality) then do USS of kidney
- Resuscitation( correct hypovolemia)
- Antipyretic(Acetaminophen)
- Monitor vitals(RR, I/O, SPO2,)

Periodic culture of the urine assists in detection of recurrence. 

Relapse is defined as recurrent infection from the same species and type-specific strain of organism present before treatment; this represents a treatment failure. Most relapses occur < 2 weeks after completion of therapy. 

Reinfection is recurrent infection due to a different strain of bacteria after successful treatment of the initial infection, occurring > 3 weeks after the completion of therapy.
