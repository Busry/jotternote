## 1. Definition
Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis) in pregnancy is a state of **excess thyroid hormone activity**, characterized biochemically by:
- **Elevated free T4 (fT4) and T3**
- **Suppressed or undetectable TSH**
### Subclinical hyperthyroidism
This is characterized by **suppressed TSH with normal T4 and T3 levels**. The source notes that it occurs in about **1.7% of screened pregnant women** and generally has no adverse effect on pregnancy, so routine screening and treatment are not warranted.

---
## 2. Prevalence
- Hyperthyroidism/thyrotoxicosis occurs in approximately **0.05–0.2% of pregnancies**.
- **Graves' disease is the commonest cause** during pregnancy.
---
## 3. Causes / Risk Factors

### Causes
1. **Graves' disease — commonest cause**
    - Caused by thyroid-stimulating antibodies (TSAb), which are IgG antibodies.
    - TSAb binds to and stimulates the **TSH receptor**, resulting in excessive thyroid hormone production.
    - TSAb can cross the placenta and cause **fetal or neonatal hyperthyroidism**.  
2. **Thyroiditis**
3. **Thyroid adenoma**
4. **Multinodular goitre**

---
## 4. Symptoms

Symptoms may include:
- **Heat intolerance**
- **Fatigue**
- **Anxiety**
- **Excessive sweating (diaphoresis)**
- **Palpitations/tachycardia**
- **Severe nausea and vomiting**
**Important:** Many symptoms overlap with normal pregnancy, making clinical diagnosis difficult.
---
## 5. Signs

### General signs
- **Pulse >100 bpm**
- **Goitre**
- **Exophthalmos**
- **Widened pulse pressure**
- Tachycardia
- Diaphoresis
### Laboratory-associated findings

May include:
- Normocytic, normochromic anaemia
- Mild neutropenia
- Elevated liver enzymes
---
# 6. Complications

## Maternal complications
The most common complication of hyperthyroidism in pregnancy is **pre-eclampsia**.
Other complications include:
- Spontaneous miscarriage
- Pregnancy-induced hypertension
- Preterm delivery
- Anaemia
- Increased susceptibility to infections
- Placental abruption
- Cardiac arrhythmias
- Congestive cardiac failure
- **Thyroid storm**

### Thyroid storm

This is a **life-threatening complication** of severe hyperthyroidism.
Features include:
- Hyperthermia/thermoregulatory dysfunction
- Agitation
- Delirium
- Coma
- Gastrointestinal dysfunction
- Severe tachycardia
- Heart failure
It may be precipitated by:
- Labour and delivery
- Caesarean section
- Infection
- Pre-eclampsia
---
## Fetal and neonatal complications
- Fetal/neonatal hyperthyroidism
- Intrauterine growth restriction
- Stillbirth
- Prematurity
- Fetal hypothyroidism due to excessive maternal thionamide treatment
- Morbidity from antithyroid medication
Approximately **1–5% of infants born to women with Graves' disease** develop hyperthyroidism at birth due to transplacental transfer of TSAb. Risk correlates with maternal TSAb titre.

### Fetal signs of hyperthyroidism
- **Fetal tachycardia >160 bpm**
- Fetal goitre
- Poor fetal growth
---
# 7. Investigations

### Thyroid function tests
The key investigations are:

|Test|Expected finding|
|---|---|
|**TSH**|↓ Suppressed/undetectable|
|**Free T4**|↑|
|**T4**|↑|
|**T3**|↑|
|**Free T3**|↑|

### Thyroid-stimulating antibody
- **TSAb titre** may be elevated, particularly in Graves' disease.
- Important because TSAb can cross the placenta and cause fetal/neonatal hyperthyroidism.
### Other investigations
May show:
- Full blood count → anaemia/neutropenia
- Liver function tests → elevated liver enzymes
### Fetal assessment
For poorly controlled hyperthyroidism or high TSAb titres:
- Serial ultrasound
- Assessment of fetal growth
- Assessment of fetal well-being
- Fetal heart rate monitoring

---

# 8. Treatment
## Goals of treatment
The objectives are to:
1. Rapidly achieve and maintain **euthyroidism**
2. Use the **minimum effective medication dose**
3. Provide symptomatic relief
4. Maintain **fT4 in the upper third of the normal range**
## A. Antithyroid drugs
The main drugs are **thionamides**:
- **Propylthiouracil (PTU)**
- **Methimazole**
Both inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis, while **PTU additionally inhibits peripheral conversion of T4 to T3**.
### Typical initial doses in the source
- Methimazole: **20–40 mg/day**
- PTU: **200–400 mg/day**
The dose is subsequently reduced as the patient improves.
### Choice according to trimester
**Before pregnancy / first trimester**
- PTU is preferred because methimazole has stronger associations with certain fetal congenital abnormalities when used in the first trimester.
**Second trimester**
- PTU may be continued or changed to methimazole.
- For hyperthyroidism diagnosed in the second trimester, either PTU or methimazole is acceptable.
### Important adverse effects
- Pruritus
- Skin rash
- Urticaria
- Fever
- Arthralgia
- Cholestatic jaundice
- Lupus-like syndrome
- Migratory polyarthritis
- **Agranulocytosis** — rare but serious
A WBC count should be obtained before treatment.
**Important distinction:**
- **PTU → hepatotoxicity/liver failure**
- **Methimazole → greater first-trimester teratogenic risk**
---
## B. β-blockers

**Propranolol 20–40 mg every 6–8 hours** may be used for severe symptoms such as tachycardia and palpitations.
It should generally be used for **short periods**, because prolonged use may be associated with:
- Intrauterine growth restriction
- Neonatal hypoglycaemia
---
## C. Surgery
Surgery is reserved for exceptional circumstances, such as:
- Allergy/intolerance to available antithyroid drugs
- Failure to respond to very large doses of medication
---
# 9. Treatment of Thyroid Storm
Thyroid storm requires **aggressive emergency treatment**.
The treatment aims to:
1. **Reduce thyroid hormone synthesis**
    - PTU or methimazole
2. **Reduce thyroid hormone release**
    - Iodine solution such as **Lugol's solution or SSKI**
3. **Reduce peripheral effects/conversion**
    - β-blocker
    - Glucocorticoids
4. **Supportive treatment**
    - IV fluid hydration
    - Nutritional support
PTU/methimazole can be administered through a nasogastric tube if there is altered consciousness. Lithium carbonate may be used instead of iodine in patients with iodine-induced anaphylaxis.
**Avoid aspirin**, as it can increase free T4 and T3 concentrations.

---
## 10. Prognosis

When hyperthyroidism is **well controlled**, maternal and fetal prognosis is generally **excellent**. Most maternal and neonatal complications occur with **untreated or poorly controlled disease**.

### Exam memory aid
**Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy = “HOT FAST GO”**
- **HOT** → Heat intolerance
- **F** → Fatigue
- **A** → Anxiety
- **S** → Sweating
- **T** → Tachycardia
- **G** → Goitre
- **O** → Ophthalmopathy/exophthalmos

**Key laboratory triad:**  
**↑ T4/T3 + ↓ TSH = overt hyperthyroidism**.
